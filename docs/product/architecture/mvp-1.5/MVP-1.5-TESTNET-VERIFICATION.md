## What blocks the real settlement (exact)
1. **Owner-run Freighter validation: DONE on 2026-11-07 (all six PASS).** Original item: **Owner-run Freighter validation** of `signAuthEntry` for an AddressV2 entry (procedure and page: `MVP-1.5-FREIGHTER-OWNER-VALIDATION.md`, `/wallet-check`). The deployed contract requires at least one such entry for any two-party session (the join).
2. **A second Testnet account**, funded and owned in the owner's Freighter, as the other participant. Public key needed; no secret.
3. **Multisig participant.** The only spendable governed supply (1 base unit) sits in the administrative multisig. Its transactions must be sourced from it and signed on the envelope by two wallet profiles (Founder 5 + Security 1 = 6). Tobin's ownership proof is a single-key signature and cannot prove control of a multisig account; a signer-set aware proof is required (design decision).
4. **Supply.** Total supply is 1 base unit; minting is not authorized. The largest settlement the existing supply can fund is 1 base unit from the administrative account. Any amount above 1, or any debtor other than the administrative account, cannot settle with the existing tokens.
Nothing was minted, changed or submitted to work around these.

## The smallest real settlement that is possible, once unblocked
Participants: the administrative account (debtor, 1 base unit) and the owner's second account X (creditor, session creator). One obligation: administrative account owes X 1. On chain: X creates the session (source credential, X signs the envelope); the administrative account joins (source = administrative account, envelope signed by Founder then Security; X's `address_v2` entry signed through Freighter), submits its obligation, finalize, propose, both approve, and the administrative account settles (source = it, envelope co-signed; the `token.transfer` of 1 unit is part of its authorization). The adapter already produces this plan; the comparison engine checks the on-chain state after each stage.
